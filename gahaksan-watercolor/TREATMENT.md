# 가학산 수목원 개장 — Watercolor treatment

Style: Watercolor Brush · 15.0 s · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 30 fps · no narration (the online Korean voice is blocked in this environment; hand-written notes and the score carry the film) · Korean notes with small English/mono labels.

## Three candidate structures

1. **One tall journal sheet, tilting down.** The page title at the top, then the five spaces painted one under another as the camera tilts down the sheet, ending on a vermilion stamp at the foot of the page. A vertical frame wants a vertical move; a single continuous sheet is the style's own grammar (no cuts).
2. **Locked page, five specimens.** One plate where the five spaces paint in around a central path, one per two seconds. Calm, but five small vignettes in 1080 px wide are too small to read at phone size.
3. **A finished painting run backwards.** The full arboretum on the page, washed back to paper, then painted forwards. Striking, but in 15 s the backwards half spends time on a picture we haven't introduced.

**Chosen: 1.** The tilt gives every space its own full-width moment and its note time to be read; the stamp at the foot answers "can I go?" in one word.

## Logline and arc

A field journal of the day Gahaksan Arboretum opened: the brush walks down the page through five gardens and signs it with a red stamp — free.

- Setup (0–2.2 s): blank warm paper; the title writes itself, a first wash of morning sky.
- Journey (2.2–12.3 s): one continuous tilt down; each space paints itself in as it rises into view, structure first (gate → flowers, dome frame → plants, trunk → four seasonal crowns, trunks → canopy, stones → moss), each with a hand-written note and leader line.
- Ending (12.3–15 s): the tilt settles on the foot of the page; the brush is lifted (music thins to the paper), a vermilion stamp thumps "무료 개방"; the last note dries.

## Benchmark

Naturalist field journals and botanical plates; long vertical scrolls read top to bottom.
- Learn: specimen + note; generous paper; structure before detail; one accent colour reserved for one recurring element (the stamp).
- Don't take: any specific artwork, the city's logo, news photos (we paint our own impressions, not the real buildings' likenesses).

## Cue map

- 84 BPM-ish gentle pulse is not used; the score is free-time: felt-piano-like plucked notes and kalimba on each painting-in cue, a soft pad under the tilt, glockenspiel on the stamp.
- Paper brush rustles on each painting-in (panned with the station's side), pencil scratches on each note, a soft stamp thump at 12.8, a near-silence 12.3–12.8 (brush lifted) before it.

## Type

Gowun Batang (serif) for the title; Nanum Pen Script (handwriting) for notes; DM Mono for small labels. No separate subtitle band: the notes are the text layer, and the `.srt` lists them.
